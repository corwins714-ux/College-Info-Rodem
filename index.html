Preface

If you are interested in alternatives to the traditional 4 year college experience and expense this info is relevant for you

The most common ways to fast track earning college credits as well as spending as little money as possible is the focus


College Credits and Costs Alternatives:

Phase 1: Earn College Credits Without Taking Classes ~ Spend Little to No Money
    • College Level Examination Program (CLEP) 대학 수준 시험
      • CLEPs are national exams granting college credit for knowledge
      • 이미 습득한 지식을 시험으로 대학 학점 인정

    • Subject Standardized Tests (DSST) 표준화 과목 시험
      • Similar to CLEP, DSST exams grant college credits and are widely accepted
      • 과 유사, 군인 및 민간 학생 모두 인정

    • Perspective｜관점
      • You can earn 30 college credits (or more) via CLEP/DSST and bypass one year of college classes
      • 일반적인 고등학교 졸업생은 CLEP/DSST 시험으로 30학점 (대학 1년치) 취득 가능 — 수업에 참석하지 않고도

    • CLEP / DSST Exams
      • These are short exams that are accepted by many colleges for credits – for example, I tested out of 30 hours of college via CLEP/DSST exams while I was in the Air Force, the 30 hours equals one year of college ~ these exams were free for me as a military benefit, but they cost over $100 each for civilians.
    • CLEP Website
    • DSST Website

    • Modern States website
      • A very rich guy funds CLEP exams using this website; this makes them cost free for civilians as well. You have to complete a short online course to prep you for CLEP exams & you send your receipts for the exams and any other fee’s and they reimburse you for the CLEP exams and admin fees at test centers:
    • 1. Complete a Modern States course to prepare for a CLEP exam
    • 2. Request a free CLEP exam voucher from Modern States
    • 3. Register for the CLEP exam through College Board
    • 4. Take the CLEP exam at an approved testing center
    • Students Should Watch For
      • CLEP restrictions - some colleges accept CLEP only for electives or general education, not for your major
      • Processing (evaluation) fees - Some private universities charge “processing fees” (or invent a bureaucratic term for it) for each CLEP you submit - which can be over $100 per CLEP; while you're still saving money - this is really irritating
      • Fine print checks - If you know what college you're applying to, you can check the school’s catalog or contact the registrar’s office for fine print before taking exams - if there are hidden rules or exceptions, you can find them here
      • Registrar runaround - some registrar offices will bounce you to the Dean of Whatever school you’re entering to get their transfer credits approval policy; sort of like they're telling you “We don’t want to give you a straight answer, and we hope you give up.”
      • Expiration rules - some colleges treat CLEP scores like they “expire” after X years - a CLEP itself never expires, but colleges sometimes impose their own internal cutoff
      • Transfer caps - colleges cap total transfer credits, typically ~60 semester hours for a four year degree - there are cool schools with much higher limits
      • Major‑specific blockages - can depend on the major or on the Dean of the school
      • Testing center admin fees - with Modern States covering the exam cost, some test centers charge additional $20–$40 admin fees, Modern States usually reimburses these - but students need to save & send them receipts
      • Official transcripts - e.g., most colleges require official transcripts to be sent directly from College Board to the college’s registrar to accept CLEP credits; they will not accept them from you personally

Phase 2: Alternative College Options
Competency-Based Education (CBE) → 역량 기반 교육
CBE Institutions use Subscription-Based Tuition: Many charge per term, e.g., you pay for a 6-month term of college class access, not per credit hours Finish faster, pay less: Since tuition is charged per term (not per credit), the faster you complete courses, the more value you get. Many students finish their degree in under 3 years. For example, at Western Governors University, students pay $4,000 for six months of "college". During the six months, you can complete as many courses as possible - there are "assessments" at the beginning of each course and if you can pass the assessment you get the credit hours and move on to the next course(s).

Phase 4: FAFSA + Ways to Pay for College
    • Application for Federal Student Aid ~ FAFSA®
      • FAFSA is simply an online form students and families complete to apply for federal, state, and school financial aid programs which could include:
      • Grants
      • Loans
      • Scholarships
      • Work-study opportunities/funds

      Note: in the "Phase 1" section, getting the first year of college free is demonstrated via CLEPs + Modern States test vouchers/reimbursements for any fees ~ in this section, using FAFSA + the Hawaiʻi Promise Scholarship, a student can get their first two years of college tuition free.
      After FAFSA, Hawaiʻi Promise covers whatever tuition, fees, books, supplies, and transportation costs remain ~ so combining FAFSA + Hawaiʻi Promise makes UH community college free for most Hawaiʻi residents.
      Federal & State Aid Options
      • Federal Subsidized Loans - need‑based federal loans • no interest while you’re in school
      • Federal Unsubsidized Loans - available to all students • interest starts immediately
      • PLUS Loans - parent or grad student loans with a credit check
      • Hawaiʻi Promise - covers tuition, fees, books, supplies, and transportation for UH community colleges; combined with FAFSA, community college is free
      • Air Force ROTC Scholarships - full or partial tuition, books, $300–$500 monthly stipend; for students earning a bachelor’s degree to become a commissioned officer in the Air Force or Space Force
Phase 5: Ways to Apply to College (Application Platforms) & Financial Tools (Save Money)
  • States with Centralized Public University Application Platforms
    • UC Application – University of California system (UC Berkeley, UCLA, etc.)
    • Cal State Apply – California State University campuses (CSU Fullerton, etc.)
    • ApplyTexas – Most public universities in Texas
    • SUNY Application – State University of New York campuses
    • GAfutures – Public colleges + HOPE Scholarship tracking
    • CFNC – College Foundation of North Carolina; includes public universities
    • UW System Application – University of Wisconsin campuses
    • Apply Idaho – Free application for Idaho public colleges
    • UAOnline – University of Alaska campuses
  • Additional & International College Application Platforms & Methods
    • QuestBridge – For high-achieving, low-income students
    • NACAC Fee Waiver – National fee waiver form
    • Common Black College Application (CBCA) – One application for 60+ HBCUs; $20 flat fee
    • Study in Korea – Official Korean government portal
    • UCAS (UK) – England’s centralized application system
    • OUAC (Ontario, Canada) – Ontario Universities Application Centre
    • UAC (Australia) – NSW centralized application system
    • JASSO (Japan) – Japanese student guidance and scholarships
    • Uni-Assist (Germany) – Centralized processing for German universities
  • Top Platforms for Applying to South Korean Universities
    • Study in Korea – Official gateway for international students
    • Korean University Information System (KUIS)
    • Direct University Portals – SNU, Yonsei, KAIST, Korea University
    • Qogent Global – Third-party application support
    • Global Scholarships Korea Guide – Step-by-step application guidance
  • Common Application & Coalition Application
    • Common App – A single online application used by 1,000+ mainland colleges, including most private universities and many public universities outside Hawaiʻi
    • Let's students apply to multiple colleges with one application
    • Stores essays, recommendation letters, activities, transcripts
    • Required by many selective mainland schools
    • Not used by UH (so irrelevant for Hawaiʻi‑only students)
    Coalition App – A smaller application platform used by ~150 colleges, originally designed to support low‑income and first‑generation students
    • Some selective schools accept it
    • Has a “Locker” tool for storing essays and projects
    • Much smaller footprint than Common App
    • Barely relevant for Hawaiʻi students
  • American Opportunity Tax Credit (AOTC)
    • A federal tax credit that helps families pay for the first four years of college expenses
    • Maximum credit: $2,500 per student, per year
    • Refundable portion: Up to $1,000 refunded, even if you owe no taxes
    • Applies to each eligible student in the household
    • Can be claimed for four tax years per student
