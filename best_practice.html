Preface

If transitioning into college world / life was a sport, this content covers some fundamentals to assist you in becoming a competent to even a really good player. For example, you can make money (actually turn a profit) on college books instead of dropping dosh at a college campus bookstore like its a charity promising to solve world hunger. You can also set yourself up now to save phat stacks of cash in the future on interest rates for major purchases like cars, houses, high-caliber home-defense weaponization, firetrucks - whatever you attach a high value to in life. At a minimum, you will have your eyes at least half open (versus being blindsided) when it comes to understanding different types of student loans, knowing deep inside your dark little hearts that "interest rates" means other stuff and not just vibing or playing eye tag.

College Books - Buying and Selling

Students can buy used or older edition books cheaply, then resell them for equal or higher value on platforms like BookScouter, eBay, Facebook Marketplace, etc. = actual profit i.e., real cash

College campus bookstores often buy back at 10–30% of the original price - Online marketplaces often pay 50–90%

Credit & Debit Cards
      • Leverage credit and debit cards to maximize credit scores, budget smart, and never pay interest
      • Credit Cards (2 kinds)
      • Student Credit Cards
      • Secured Credit Cards

      My Recommendations

      • Students get their own checking account/debit card under a parent’s account (joint, whatever) so the parent can transfer $$ if needed
      • Students get the Discover it® Student Cash Back card for the first year of college and use the cash-back rewards
      • Students set up debit card auto-payment(s) (scheduled around the 5th day of the month; charges can take 1-3 business days to fully post) to pay off the credit card balance after the recurring charges hit
      • Students set up credit card auto-payment(s) (scheduled around the 1st day of the month) on the credit card to pay all recurring bills like Netflix or whatever
      • Start with a student credit card or a secured card if you have no credit history
      • Use the card for predictable expenses (subscriptions, groceries, etc.)
      • Pay off the full balance each month to avoid interest
      • Set up autopay so you never miss a due date
      • Monitor your credit score with free tools like Credit Karma or your bank’s app

      Why do these - what are the benefits:

      • You have a backup if you lose one of your cards
      • You never pay credit card interest
      • Builds up your credit score (rating) - payment history & low credit utilization make up 35% and 30% of your credit score respectively

      Why is credit score so important?

      • Home Loans (Mortgages) 760+ gets a mortgage rate around 6.45%, 620 = up to 8.05%
      • Auto Loans 720+ can get under 5%, 600s = 10% or more

      Bottomline; a credit score of 720+ versus 620 or lower will save you a few hundred thousand dollars on a 30 year home mortgage & will save you over $10k on a $60k car loan

      Student Credit Cards

      • Designed specifically for college students with little or no credit history
      • No security deposit required
      • Often come with no annual fee
      • May offer cash-back rewards and perks like bonuses for good grades
      • Require proof of college enrollment
      • You may need independent income or a co-signer if you're under 21
      • Great if you're a student with some income and want to start building credit while earning small rewards

      Bank of Hawaii - Bankoh Student Credit Card

      • Local bank with branches across Hawaii
      • Fraud protection and mobile banking
      • Accessible online application and in-person support

      Discover it® Student Cash Back

      • No annual fee, 5% cash back in rotating categories
      • Excellent access features & screen reader friendly app
      • Available to Hawaii residents nationwide
      • $20 statement credit each school year for good grades (GPA of 3.0 or higher) for up to 5 years
      • One-time bonus: end of your first year, Discover will match every dollar of cash back you earned

      Secured Credit Cards

      • Ideal for anyone (student or not) who’s starting from scratch or rebuilding credit
      • Requires a refundable deposit (usually $200–$500) that becomes your credit limit
      • Easier to get approved for—even with no credit history
      • Doesn’t require student status
      • Can be upgraded to an unsecured card after responsible use
      • Perfect if you want a guaranteed way to start building credit and don’t mind putting down a deposit

      American Savings Bank - Secured Credit Card

      • Hawaii-based bank with strong community ties
      • Requires refundable deposit ($300–$5,000)
      • Accessible customer service and in-branch support

      Capital One - Secured Mastercard

      • Requires refundable deposit ($49–$200)
      • Credit tracking and upgrade path to unsecured card
      • Mobile app works well with accessibility tools

      Which One’s Better?

      • If you're a student with income and want perks → go for a student card
      • If you're just starting out or don’t qualify for a student card → a secured card is a solid path
Student Loans
    • Direct Subsidized Loans (Undergraduate)
      • Description: The government pays interest until you stop going to college
      • Interest Rate: 6.52%
      • Interest is paid by the government while you're in school at least half-time, during grace periods, and deferments
    • Direct Unsubsidized Loans (Undergraduate)
      • Description: You start paying interest immediately
      • Interest Rate: 6.52%
      • Interest accrues from the time the loan is disbursed - even while you're in school
    • Direct Unsubsidized Loans (Graduate/Professional)
      • Description: You start paying interest immediately
      • Interest Rate: 8.07%
      • No subsidized option for graduate students; interest accrues immediately
    • Parent PLUS Loans
      • Description: You start paying interest immediately
      • Interest Rate: 9.07%
      • Rates based on 10-Year Treasury Note yield from May 2025 + fixed margin; credit check required
    • Grad PLUS Loans
      • Description: You start paying interest immediately
      • Interest Rate: 9.07%
      • Rates based on 10-Year Treasury Note yield from May 2025 + fixed margin; credit check required
Establishing & Increasing Credit Score
    • Credit Score - Credit Card to Debit Card or Bank Account Flow Strategy
      • Step 1: Set up auto‑payments on your credit card for recurring bills e.g., Netflix, subscriptions, rent, insurance
      • Step 2: Let the charges hit, then wait 1–3 business days for each charge to fully post
      • Step 3: On day 4, pay off the credit card using a bank debit card or linked bank account
      • Step 4: Before the end of the month, verify your bills were all paid and there were no problems or errors
Payment history on credit cards accounts for 35% of your FICO score and credit history length is also a major factor in your credit score
Using a small amount of your credit limit (and paying it off monthly) keeps your utilization ratio low - another key score booster
